\begin{abstract}
Can a smaller language model continue from a runtime key--value (KV) state
produced by a larger model without re-prefilling the original context?  We
study this question as \emph{cross-model state handoff} and separate three
claims that are often conflated: engineering replay correctness,
representation compatibility, and downstream capability transfer.  On one
real-GPU Qwen3-4B$\rightarrow$Qwen3-1.7B run, external replay of the student's
own KV state is exact on four samples.  A ridge map trained on 64 prompts and
evaluated on 16 disjoint prompts obtains key cosine $0.9661$ and key
$R^2=0.9282$, but value cosine is only $0.5772$ and value $R^2=-0.9449$.
These measurements support an asymmetric linear-compatibility result; they do
not establish task retention, capability gain, latency savings, or
generalization.  We identify two evaluation errors in the original pipeline:
global cropping reduced the nominal 512/1024-token experiment to 70 tokens,
and a value diagnostic incorrectly used values as both keys and values.  We
provide corrected variable-length evaluation, standard
$\operatorname{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt d)V$ diagnostics, and evidence gates
that prevent synthetic scores or proxy timing from entering scientific
verdicts.  Based on these findings, we propose the most viable next path: learn
a functional compatibility base first, then train a bounded advantage residual
against real held-out task loss.  The present evidence supports continuing the
research program, but not yet claiming runtime capability transfer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Transformer inference materializes a KV cache during prefill and reuses it
during autoregressive decoding \citep{vaswani2017attention}.  The cache is a
major serving object: systems optimize its allocation \citep{kwon2023vllm},
quantization \citep{liu2024kivi}, token selection \citep{li2024snapkv}, and
reuse within a model \citep{yao2024cacheblend}.  A harder question is whether a
runtime state produced by one model can be consumed by another.

We consider a large teacher $T$, a smaller student $S$, a long context $X$,
and a short continuation query $q$.  The teacher has already produced
$C_T(X)$, while the student is not allowed to read $X$ again.  A handoff method
constructs
\begin{equation}
  \widehat C_S(X)=F_\theta(C_T(X)),
\end{equation}
injects it into the student, and evaluates the student's continuation.  This
setting is attractive for mid-session model downgrade, repeated reuse, and
heterogeneous serving.  It is also easy to evaluate incorrectly: high KV
cosine need not imply equivalent logits, equivalent logits need not imply task
quality, and a CUDA microbenchmark need not imply end-to-end savings.

This paper makes three contributions.  First, it introduces an evidence ladder
for cross-model handoff and treats missing evidence as an inconclusive result,
not as a failed or successful transfer.  Second, it reports a real-GPU
large-to-small mapping result and diagnoses the K/V asymmetry and two protocol
errors that materially affect interpretation.  Third, it presents a staged
experimental design centered on functional compatibility followed by a real
task-trained advantage residual.  Our conclusion is deliberately narrow:
cross-model linear state compatibility is plausible for keys in the tested
pair, while runtime capability transfer remains unmeasured.

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
The closest concurrent work is no longer only generic KV-cache systems.
Heo et al. study cross-model KV transfer within model families using
closed-form per-head ridge maps, layer selection, and de-RoPE, and report
receiver task retention and prefill speedups across multiple pairs
\citep{heo2026crossmodel}.  Cache-to-Cache (C2C) learns a neural projection,
fusion, and gating mechanism for semantic communication between different
LLMs \citep{fu2025c2c}.  Mixture-of-Translators extends cache translation to
heterogeneous models with multiple translation experts and target-side
correction \citep{lee2026mot}.  These studies directly establish that
cross-model cache mapping is an active and feasible research direction.

Our scope is different and narrower.  We do not claim to originate
cross-model KV transfer, ridge translation, or de-RoPE mapping.  Instead, this
paper audits a preliminary large-to-small replication/extension and asks what
its artifacts can actually support.  Its contribution is the separation of
representation recovery, injected task retention, capability gain, and
end-to-end systems benefit; a corrected K/V functional diagnostic; and an
artifact-level gate that prevents lower-grade evidence from silently
satisfying a higher-grade claim.  A future competitive evaluation must compare
directly with the ridge protocol of Heo et al., C2C, and a heterogeneous
translator such as MoT under the same zero-prefill receiver constraint.

\section{Problem Formulation and Evidence Levels}
\subsection{Three distinct targets}
We distinguish the following targets.

\paragraph{Replay correctness.}
The student's native self-prefill cache is exported, reconstructed, and
replayed.  Matching logits and tokens test cache capture and injection, but do
not involve cross-model mapping.

\paragraph{Compatibility or replacement.}
A mapped teacher state should approximate the behavior obtained from student
self-prefill.  The primary metric must be downstream task retention,
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{task}}=
  \frac{\operatorname{Score}(S\mid\widehat C_S(X),q)}
       {\operatorname{Score}(S\mid C_S(X),q)}.
\end{equation}
KV cosine, $R^2$, CKA, and attention-output cosine are diagnostics, not
substitutes for $R_{\mathrm{task}}$.

\paragraph{Capability transfer.}
The stronger claim requires the handed-off student to outperform student
self-prefill on a preregistered held-out task:
\begin{align}
  \chg &= \operatorname{Score}(S\mid\widehat C_S)-
          \operatorname{Score}(S\mid C_S),\\
  \tgrr &=
  \frac{\operatorname{Score}(S\mid\widehat C_S)-
        \operatorname{Score}(S\mid C_S)}
       {\operatorname{Score}(T\mid C_T)-
        \operatorname{Score}(S\mid C_S)}.
\end{align}
Positive $\chg$ with a confidence interval excluding zero is the primary
scientific endpoint.  Positive $\tgrr$ indicates recovery of part of the
teacher--student gap.

\subsection{Evidence gate}
Every artifact is assigned one of four roles: measured task evidence, measured
representation evidence, proxy evidence, or synthetic/placeholder evidence.
A claim is eligible only if all required inputs have the appropriate role.

\section{Method: Functional Compatibility Before Advantage}
The most viable path separates a stable compatibility base from a later
advantage-carrying residual.

\subsection{Stage I: compatibility base}
For student layer $\ell$ and head $h$, selected teacher layers are mixed and
mapped separately for keys and values:
\begin{align}
  Z_{\ell h}^{K} &= \sum_{i\in\mathcal A(\ell)}w^{K}_{\ell i h}K^T_{ih},
  &\widehat K^S_{\ell h} &= Z_{\ell h}^{K}W^K_{\ell h},\\
  Z_{\ell h}^{V} &= \sum_{i\in\mathcal A(\ell)}w^{V}_{\ell i h}V^T_{ih},
  &\widehat V^S_{\ell h} &= Z_{\ell h}^{V}W^V_{\ell h}.
\end{align}
Keys are de-rotated before mapping and re-rotated in the student coordinate
system.  Values are not de-rotated.  Ridge regression is a useful calibration
baseline, but the training target should progress from raw KV reconstruction
to functional behavior:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{compat}}=
\lambda_{\mathrm{task}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{task}}+
\lambda_{\mathrm{KL}}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{self}}\Vert p_{\mathrm{handoff}})+
\lambda_{\mathrm{att}}\lVert A_{\mathrm{self}}-A_{\mathrm{handoff}}\rVert_2^2+
\lambda_{\mathrm{KV}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{KV}}.
\end{equation}
The task and logit terms are primary; the KV term regularizes but does not
define success.

\subsection{Correct functional diagnostic}
Given student queries $Q^S$, the diagnostic must use a causal and padding-aware
mask $M$, with disallowed entries set to $-\infty$:
\begin{equation}
 A(Q,K,V;M)=\operatorname{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt d+M)V.
\end{equation}
We report three interventions: K-only
$A(Q,\widehat K,V^S;M)$, V-only $A(Q,K^S,\widehat V;M)$, and joint
$A(Q,\widehat K,\widehat V;M)$.  The corrected implementation applies the
causal mask by default and accepts an explicit key-validity mask.  Random
queries are explicitly labeled proxy; the final experiment records real
student queries and their native attention masks.

\subsection{Stage II: bounded advantage residual}
Only after the base reaches stable real task retention do we train an advantage
residual:
\begin{equation}
  C_S^*=C_{\mathrm{base}}+\alpha_{\max}\tanh(a)\odot R_{\mathrm{adv}}(C_T).
\end{equation}
$R_{\mathrm{adv}}$ uses separate low-rank K/V branches and RMS calibration.
Unlike the preliminary implementation, $a$ must be updated by real task loss;
an all-zero, never-trained $\alpha$ is not evidence for or against advantage
transfer.  We recommend causal layer/head interventions: ablate or inject one
candidate subspace at a time and measure paired changes in $\chg$.  This turns
``advantage'' from a name into a testable, task-causal object.

\section{Real-GPU Experimental Evidence}
\subsection{Setup}
The tested pair is Qwen3-4B$\rightarrow$Qwen3-1.7B
\citep{yang2025qwen3}.  Both expose eight KV heads with head dimension 128;
the teacher and student have 36 and 28 layers, respectively.  Models are
loaded sequentially on one GPU.  The real mapping run uses 64 calibration
prompts and 16 disjoint evaluation prompts.  K and V maps are fitted
independently with ridge coefficient $10^{-3}$.  The archived run requested
512/1024-token contexts but the old evaluator cropped every sample to the
global minimum, 70 tokens.  We therefore treat the result as a short-context
pilot and require a corrected rerun before any long-context claim.

The key result is encouraging: a simple per-head linear map recovers much of
the student key representation on disjoint prompts.  The value result is the
dominant bottleneck.  Negative $R^2$ means the fitted value map is worse than a
mean predictor under this metric, even though its cosine is positive.  We do
not interpret $0.5772$ as a mathematical ceiling.  In particular, near-zero
raw cross-model cosine does not preclude a learned rotation or a nonlinear
functional map.

The archived attention-output values are excluded from the scientific table.
The old code evaluated each channel through a two-argument helper that
implicitly set $V=K$; consequently the ``V attention'' branch computed
$A(Q,V,V)$ rather than $A(Q,K,V;M)$.  Corrected code now reports K-only,
V-only, and joint causal interventions and marks deterministic random $Q$ as
proxy evidence.

\subsection{What the run does not establish}
The run does not measure real injected downstream task retention.  The
reported ``retention'' was KV cosine divided by self-cosine, whose denominator
is one by construction.  Synthetic T09 scores therefore cannot establish
positive $\chg$ or $\tgrr$; CUDA proxy timing cannot establish positive
$\psr$; placeholder geometry cannot explain the failure; and two executions
of the same Qwen pair cannot establish two-pair generalization.  Under the
evidence gate, the current verdict is \textbf{inconclusive evidence}, not
runtime capability transfer and not a definitive stop result.

\section{Revised Experimental Design}
\subsection{Phase A: make compatibility functional}
Use 100--500 calibration prompts and at least 500 held-out task examples.
Preserve each prompt's natural length up to the requested context cap; aggregate
ridge sufficient statistics across variable-length samples.  Evaluate 512,
1024, 4096, and 8192-token buckets separately.  The student self-prefill path
is the reference; the handoff path must have zero original-context prefill.

The minimum method set is student self-prefill, teacher inference, text
handoff, ridge K/V, a small nonlinear or low-rank base, and selective
recompute.  For every method report task score, task retention, next-token KL,
top-$k$ overlap, and judge/ranking consistency where applicable.  Freeze layer
mapping, ridge coefficients, ranks, and all thresholds on train/validation.

\subsection{Phase B: localize the value failure}
Run four controlled interventions: native K/native V, mapped K/native V,
native K/mapped V, and mapped K/mapped V.  Record real queries and per-layer
attention outputs.  \Needspace{8\baselineskip}Sweep value objectives in this order:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item ridge reconstruction;
  \item low-rank plus bias and scale calibration;
  \item attention-output distillation with native K;
  \item next-token KL and held-out task loss;
  \item a small token/layer recomputation budget.
\end{enumerate}
If K-only remains stable but V-only fails, allocate capacity to V rather than
increasing a shared mapper.  If failures concentrate in a few layers, selective
recompute is a stronger systems path than forcing a universal map.

\subsection{Phase C: test advantage only after Gate 1}
Require real task retention $\geq0.90$ on held-out data before training the
advantage residual.  Train on a dataset-level teacher-advantage task selected
before test evaluation; never filter test examples by teacher success.  Use at
least three seeds and paired bootstrap or permutation inference on per-example
$\chg$.  The capability gate requires: $\chg>0$, a 95\% confidence interval
whose lower bound is above zero, $\tgrr>0$, stable KL/JCR, and replication on a
second unique model pair.

\subsection{Phase D: measure systems claims end to end}
Time complete paths with synchronization, warmup, and repeated trials.  For
natural handoff, report
\begin{equation}
\psr=1-\frac{T_{\mathrm{map}}+T_{\mathrm{load}}+T_{q}}
                  {T_{\mathrm{prefill},S}(X)}.
\end{equation}
If the teacher runs only for transfer, include teacher prefill.  For repeated
reuse, report the measured break-even query count.  Include CPU offload,
host-to-device transfer, cache conversion, injection, failures, and any
fallback recomputation.  CUDA proxy kernels remain engineering diagnostics.

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{Most viable research path.}
The data argue against immediately adding a complex ``advantage'' module.  The
highest-probability path is a functional compatibility base with independent
K/V capacity and selective recomputation, followed by a small task-trained
residual.  This path is useful under all outcomes.  If compatibility and
$\chg$ both succeed, it supports runtime capability transfer.  If compatibility
succeeds but $\chg\leq0$, it yields a clean boundary: state compatibility does
not imply capability compatibility.  If values remain incompatible, the work
becomes a mechanistic study of cross-model state geometry and the minimum
recomputation needed for handoff.

\paragraph{Why the negative value result is valuable.}
The K/V asymmetry narrows the design space.  It suggests that capacity should
not be allocated uniformly and that raw reconstruction is not the only useful
target.  It also motivates layerwise interventions, because value tensors
carry the content aggregated by attention and may be more model-specific than
keys in this pair.

\paragraph{Relation to KV-cache systems work.}
Existing systems demonstrate that KV state is a first-class memory and reuse
object \citep{kwon2023vllm,liu2024kivi,li2024snapkv,yao2024cacheblend}.  Our
question is orthogonal: whether a state can cross a model boundary while
remaining functionally consumable.  Efficient attention kernels such as
FlashAttention \citep{dao2022flashattention} reduce attention I/O but do not
solve semantic cross-model compatibility.

Direct cross-model cache transfer is addressed by concurrent ridge,
cache-communication, and translator approaches
\citep{heo2026crossmodel,fu2025c2c,lee2026mot}.  Our revised experiment should
therefore be read as a rigorously gated replication and boundary study, not as
an isolated first demonstration.  The key open comparison is whether a
functional objective and selective recomputation improve the specific weak-V
regime observed here while preserving the zero-prefill receiver contract.

\section{Limitations and Reproducibility Status}
Only one model pair has real mapping measurements.  Replay correctness uses
four samples; the mapped evaluation uses 16 held-out prompts; the effective
sequence length is only 70 tokens due to the superseded global-cropping
protocol.  Real task injection scores, real student queries, end-to-end timing,
real geometry, multi-turn stability, and a second model pair are absent.  The
reported values are therefore preliminary engineering and representation
evidence.  The revised code records per-sample effective lengths, separates
metric semantics, blocks non-measured verdicts, rejects real T08 execution
until task supervision is connected, loads real geometry only from explicit
hidden-state artifacts, and groups generalization runs by model identity and
revision without discarding repeats.
For real-score execution, the revised pipeline accepts only audited held-out
artifacts with exact sample IDs, verified zero receiver prefill, and verified
task scoring.  System evidence likewise requires synchronized end-to-end
handoff records with warmup and at least ten repeats.  Generalization aggregates
all runs for each model ID plus revision, requires at least three unique seeds,
and conservatively fails a pair if any included measured run fails.  These
paths make the missing experiments executable, but no such new real artifacts
are included in the present result table.

\section{Conclusion}
The current evidence supports continued investigation, but not the claim that
runtime capability has already been transferred.  Exact self-KV replay shows
that the injection mechanism can work.  Strong held-out key recovery shows
that part of the large-to-small state relation is linearly compatible.  Weak
value recovery, a short effective context, and the absence of real downstream
and end-to-end measurements leave the central hypothesis unresolved.  The
most feasible next experiment is a functional, independently parameterized K/V
compatibility base evaluated by real task behavior, with selective recompute as
a safety valve; only then should a bounded, task-trained advantage residual be
tested.  This evidence-gated formulation makes both positive and negative
outcomes scientifically useful.

\section{Preregistered Decision Rules}
Gate 0 passes only when external replay matches native self-prefill logits and
tokens within numerical tolerance.  Gate 1 is based on real task retention:
$\geq0.90$ passes, $0.80$--$0.90$ is conditional, and $<0.80$ fails.  A
representation-only run is inconclusive regardless of cosine.  Gate 2 requires
positive real held-out $\chg$ and $\tgrr$, a paired 95\% interval above zero,
and stable behavior.  A systems claim additionally requires end-to-end timing.
Generalization requires at least two unique model-and-revision identities,
at least three unique seeds per pair, and aggregation of every included run;
it is not satisfied by two run IDs or by selecting the best repeat.

\section{Required Artifact Schema}
Each task artifact stores \texttt{offline\_demo}, \texttt{evidence\_grade},
metric semantics, model identifiers, data split identifiers, sample counts,
effective sequence-length statistics, and timing provenance.  The final
decision artifact lists all blockers rather than substituting zero for missing
measurements.  This schema is intended to make accidental overclaiming visible
in both automated reports and paper tables.

The task-score artifact additionally records the held-out split, exact sample
IDs, method, seed, score, decision, and explicit
\texttt{zero\_prefill\_verified} and
\texttt{task\_scoring\_verified} flags.  The timing artifact records synchronized
per-context, per-seed teacher prefill, student prefill, map, load, and query
times together with warmup and repeat counts.
