\begin{abstract}
Cross-model cache translation promises to remove target-side prefill by mapping
the key--value (\kv) cache a large teacher builds over a context into the cache
space of a smaller student. Reported results conflate three questions that can
fail independently: whether the injection pipeline consumes a foreign cache
correctly (\emph{mechanics}), whether a mapper replaces the student's own cache
at replacement level (\emph{mapping}), and whether the teacher's cache carries
content the frozen student can exploit (\emph{exploitability}). We audit all
three under strict zero re-prefill on Qwen3-4B$\to$1.7B and three further Qwen3
scale pairs (four in total), using per-sample identity controls, disjoint
calibration and evaluation splits, and gold-letter-probability metrics with
bootstrap intervals. Mechanics passes to a numerical tolerance: every evaluated
sample of the identity injection has logit cosine $\ge 0.9998$ against the
student's own prefill (observed minimum $0.99986$, mean $0.99998$), with a change
of $+0.0001$ $[-0.0006,+0.0009]$.
No translator we test replaces the strong student's own prefill. The audit
protocol records $133$ translation configurations across all tested pairs; on
the primary strong-student pair the largest point estimate is $-0.138$, and
every interval there lies below zero except the best one, which spans it. Controls locate the deficit:
calibration volume from $10$ to $500$ examples moves the result by $0.02$,
removing the analytic anchor of our most architecture-aware translator improves
perplexity without improving answers, and a $37$M-parameter joint MLP does not
help. Aligning our implementation with the closest published design---concatenating
the selected source layers and calibrating on $1{,}024$-token web-text
passages---leaves the verdict unchanged ($-0.223$ to $-0.279$). The weak $0.6$B
student reaches $+0.010$ $[-0.089,+0.106]$, which establishes neither
degradation nor the reporting margin we adopt ($\epsilon{=}0.02$).
Teacher-content probes never produce a gain whose interval excludes zero, and the
family-wise $95\%$ bootstrap upper estimate over the six confirmatory probe
configurations is $+0.009$. Two decouplings survive: better \kv{}
reconstruction (held-out $R^2$ $0.854$ for keys, $0.481$ for values) does not
improve answers, and near-native perplexity does not either.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Serving systems increasingly reuse computation across models: the same long
context is prefilled by several models at different scales. Because a \kv{}
cache is model-specific, a second model must normally prefill the shared prefix
again. Cache translation attacks this by fitting a map from the teacher's stored
cache into the student's cache space, after which the student answers a short
query from the translated cache alone---\emph{strict zero
re-prefill}~\citep{c2c2025,lsc2026,mot2026,heo2026,cachebridge2026,contextreuse2026}.
Reported outcomes now span a wide range: near-native retention on Qwen3
transfer directions~\citep{cachebridge2026}, accuracy above the receiving
model's own baseline on LongBench2~\citep{contextreuse2026}, and large deficits
elsewhere.

This spread is hard to interpret because published evaluations score an
end-to-end pipeline against a student baseline, which conflates three questions
that can fail independently. Does the injection pipeline consume a foreign
cache correctly (\emph{mechanics})? Does the map replace the student's own cache
at replacement level (\emph{mapping})? Does the teacher's cache carry content
the frozen student can exploit at all (\emph{exploitability})? A positive delta
could come from any of the three, and a null result cannot be attributed
without instruments that isolate them.

We therefore evaluate cache translation as an audit with three gated
hypotheses and one instrument each.
\textbf{H1 (mechanics)}: injecting the student's own cache through the full
translation-and-injection pipeline reproduces the student's own prefill.
\textbf{H2 (mapping)}: some tested translator reaches replacement level, scored
by the change in gold-letter probability over the student's own prefill.
\textbf{H3 (exploitability)}: tested teacher-derived cache content provides an
incremental benefit when introduced into an otherwise native student cache.

Our contribution is the audit itself and what it returns, not a new translator.
Three results follow, and each is bounded by the designs we tested.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mechanics is not the failure mode.} The identity control
reproduces the student to numerical tolerance (mean per-sample logit cosine
$0.99998$, minimum $0.99986$ over evaluated samples; change $+0.0001$
$[-0.0006,+0.0009]$), and an offline/online pair that reloads only the persisted
cache and mapper agrees to the last recorded digit on the translated and
identity paths.
\item \textbf{No tested mapper replaces the strong student's cache.} Linear,
per-layer, task-aware, architecture-anchored, and gradient-trained
translators, calibration budgets from $10$ to $500$ examples, and the closest
published design in both its averaged and concatenated form all fail: the
largest point estimate on the strong pair is $-0.138$ $[-0.312,+0.037]$ and
every other interval lies below zero. The weak $0.6$B student reaches $+0.010$
$[-0.089,+0.106]$, which establishes neither degradation nor our reporting
margin.
\item \textbf{No teacher-content probe establishes gain.} Mixing teacher-derived
content into the student's own cache degrades monotonically with the teacher
fraction, replacing the top third of layers is neutral rather than helpful, and
no probe configuration produces an interval that excludes zero on the positive
side.
\end{itemize}

Two further results sharpen the negative. First, improving \kv{}
reconstruction does not improve answers: a joint MLP that reaches held-out
$R^2$ of $0.854$ (keys) and $0.481$ (values), against $0.810$ and $0.323$ for
the per-head affine mapper, still lands in the same CHG band. Second, repairing
perplexity does not repair the answer deficit: a translator whose suffix
perplexity is within $2.5$ of the identity control answers $26.7\%$ against the
student's $50.0\%$. Reconstruction fidelity and fluency are therefore not
substitutes for task quality in this setting.

\textbf{Scope.} We measure a negative result about the designs we tested, not
an impossibility claim. Our primary pair is one model family, four-choice
classification with $n$ between $10$ and $100$ evaluation samples per run, and
the reporting margin $\epsilon$ was selected after the gate form was frozen
(Section~\ref{sec:framework}). Section~\ref{sec:limitations} lists what this
leaves open.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:survey}

\textbf{\kv{} reuse within one model.} Prompt caching, cache-augmented
generation, and streaming attention reuse a model's own cache: streaming
attention identifies early positions with outsized attention
mass~\citep{streamingsink2023}; CacheGen compresses and streams a
cache~\citep{cachegen2024}; CacheBlend fuses cached fragments for
retrieval-augmented generation~\citep{cacheblend2025}; KVSharer shares caches
across similar layers~\citep{kvsharer2024}. These systems assume one model on
both sides, so their question is storage and attention cost rather than
representation compatibility.

\textbf{Cross-model cache translation.} C2C translates caches directly with a
depth-ratio layer map~\citep{c2c2025}; LSC aligns caches through a shared latent
space~\citep{lsc2026}; MoT combines gated translators over depth-ratio channel
windows with a correction pass over the context~\citep{mot2026}; KVComm shares
attention-ranked layers~\citep{kvcomm2025}; Interlat communicates last hidden
states~\citep{interlat2025}; HCache restores caches from hidden-state
translations~\citep{hcache2025}. The closest strict-zero-re-prefill design fits
a closed-form linear map from selected source layers, with de-RoPE keys, and
reports $73$--$98\%$ retention on matched-\kv{} pairs~\citep{heo2026}.
Two recent entries widen the range of outcomes: CacheBridge replaces full-head
mapping with architecture-indexed support---each target head matched to one
source head, with attention-aligned calibration---and reports $99.83\%$ mean
target retention on Qwen3 with an order of magnitude less calibration
data~\citep{cachebridge2026}; a universal context-reuse layer reports that
translated \kv{} states raise LongBench2 accuracy from $27.59\%$ to $34.48\%$
for Qwen2.5-7B$\to$1.5B, $6.89$ points above the receiver's own
prefill~\citep{contextreuse2026}. Retention measures how closely a translated
cache reproduces the receiver's own prefill, not whether the receiver gains over
it, and neither of these reports isolates mechanics with an identity control.
Taken together with the deficits we measure, the published evidence now
contains near-native retention, above-baseline gain, and severe degradation,
which is the case for a shared audit rather than for or against any one
translator.

\textbf{Cross-model representation alignment.} Model stitching connects frozen
networks with a learned layer~\citep{stitching2021}; relative representations
enable zero-shot latent communication~\citep{relrep2022}; the Platonic
representation hypothesis and its refinements argue for convergent
representations across models~\citep{platonic2024,perfectplatonic2025,aristotelian2026};
embedding-space methods align vocabularies post hoc~\citep{vec2vec2025}. This
line supports a shared geometry but does not measure whether it carries a
specific task's answer information.

\textbf{Frozen-model controllability.} Task vectors, activation steering, and
tuned lenses show that a frozen model's residual stream can be steered or
read~\citep{taskvectors2023,actsteer2024,tunedlens2023,gist2023}. Our probes are
the \kv{}-cache analogue of these interventions, and they come back empty on the
pairs we test. FFN layers are key--value memories~\citep{ffnmemory2021}, which is
one reason a weight-mediated capability gap need not survive as a cache-mediated
one.

\textbf{What this line does not establish.} Across these lines, no cited
evaluation (i) separates mechanics, mapping, and exploitability, (ii) injects
the student's own cache as an identity control, (iii) reports accuracy at
matched perplexity, or (iv) constrains exploitability with a
no-additional-training probe of teacher-derived content. This audit supplies those instruments.

\section{Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Setting and metric}
A teacher $T$ prefills a context $x$ and stores a cache
$C_T(x)=\{K^T_\ell,V^T_\ell\}_{\ell=1}^{L_T}$ with $H$ \kv{} heads and head
dimension $d$. A translator $g_\theta$ maps it to $\widehat C_S(x)$ in the space
of a student $S$. The student answers a query $q$ from
$\widehat C_S(x)$ alone, under strict zero re-prefill: no context token enters
the student's forward pass. Three references anchor every comparison: the
\emph{student self-prefill} $S(q\mid C_S(x))$, the \emph{identity injection}
that feeds $C_S(x)$ through the injection pipeline, and the \emph{teacher
full-prefill} reference $T(q\mid C_T(x))$.

We score answer quality by the gold-letter probability $\gold$: the softmax over
the four answer-letter logits at the final prompt position, using each model's
own tokenizer id for each letter. Only four-choice examples are retained, so a
student with no signal sits at $0.25$. Throughout, \emph{CHG} is the change in
$\gold$ over the student's own prefill on the same examples, with $95\%$
bootstrap intervals. All intervals use a paired percentile bootstrap over
evaluation examples with $10^4$ resamples, including the family-wise probe
bound in Section~\ref{sec:h3}. Figure~\ref{fig:framework} summarizes the
setting and the three instruments.

\subsection{Three hypotheses, three instruments, three gates}
\textbf{H1 (mechanics).} The identity injection matches the student's own
prefill per sample. \emph{Gate}: every evaluated sample has logit cosine
$\ge 0.9998$ against the student's own prefill. The tolerance is orders of
magnitude away from every non-identity method we observe, whose cosines sit near
$0.5$; we report the observed minimum alongside the gate so that the criterion
is reproducible rather than an equality to one. H1 failing would mean the
pipeline, not the map, is broken.

\textbf{H2 (mapping).} Some tested translator reaches replacement level.
\emph{Gate}: the interval lower bound of CHG is above $-\epsilon$, with
$\epsilon=0.02$ gold probability---a \emph{reporting margin} selected after the
gate form was frozen, about $4\%$ of the $0.503$ student baseline, and not a
pre-registered constant. A stricter gain gate additionally requires the lower
bound to exceed $0$. We report two judgements per translated row, because they
are conceptually independent: \emph{replacement} (\emph{pass} iff the interval
lower bound is above $-\epsilon$, otherwise \emph{fail}) and \emph{direction}
(\emph{degraded} if the interval upper bound is below zero, \emph{gain} if the
lower bound is above zero, and \emph{neutral} otherwise). A row can therefore be
practically non-inferior and statistically worse than the baseline at the same
time; Table~\ref{tab:main} reports both judgements, and no row we record falls in
that overlap.

\textbf{H3 (exploitability).} Tested teacher-derived cache content provides an
incremental benefit when introduced into an otherwise native student cache.
\emph{Gate}: some probe configuration improves over the student's own cache with
an interval that excludes zero on the positive side. The probe does not train a
translator, so a positive effect would show that teacher-derived content is
exploitable under controlled interventions; a null result constrains the
extraction paths we test without excluding every nonlinear one. The gate is
therefore about tested content, not about the intrinsic information content of
the teacher's cache, and it is not specific to the end-to-end score of any
single mapper.

\subsection{Evidence tiers}
\label{sec:tiers}
The audit grew while revising, so we label every claim by how it entered the
protocol. \emph{Confirmatory} claims were fixed before the runs: the identity
gate \emph{in form} (its numerical tolerance $0.9998$ is a revision-added
operationalization, Appendix~\ref{app:repro}), the non-inferiority
\emph{form} of the replacement gate and the
$\epsilon$-free gain gate on the primary pair, and the fraction and window
probes under the rule above. \emph{Frozen-gate extensions} were added while
revising and scored under the same gates and metrics: the residual-anchored and
MLP translators, the $8$B teacher, the calibration ladder, the
cross-architecture and token-aligned students, the $1$K retrieval task, the
octant sweep, and the refit-variance study. \emph{Exploratory} analyses were
derived from the recorded runs afterwards and are worded as interpretations. We
also report a joint bootstrap over the confirmatory family in
Section~\ref{sec:h3}, since no wider correction was pre-specified.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\textbf{Models and pairs.} The primary pair is Qwen3-4B (teacher) to Qwen3-1.7B
(student)~\citep{qwen3}. We additionally run Qwen3-4B$\to$Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-8B$\to$Qwen3-1.7B,
and Qwen3-8B$\to$Qwen3-0.6B, plus five alternative students under the same
protocol: Llama-3.2-1B, Llama-3.2-3B, Gemma-2-2B, Gemma-3-1B, and Qwen2.5-1.5B.

\textbf{Data.} Calibration and evaluation use four-choice items from HellaSwag
and ARC with disjoint splits. The context-length check uses a four-choice
needle-in-a-haystack task packed to a target length; the $4$K and $8$K runs
store caches in float16, which we flag wherever those numbers appear.

\textbf{Protocol.} Every translated row is produced under zero re-prefill, with
the identity control present in the same run, a unified prompt format across
models, native rescaling of the injected cache, and per-run recording of the git
hash, protocol version, and per-sample scores. The main evaluation set for the
comparisons above is a fixed tail of $100$ HellaSwag/ARC items; the mapper ladder
uses $n=30$ or $n=63$ items depending on the calibration budget, and we state $n$
for every number we quote.

\textbf{Translators.} We test per-head ridge; per-head affine (centered, the
strongest linear family); per-layer affine; a task-aware objective; a
residual-anchored translator (RAT) built from the two
models' own projections and a shared vocabulary alignment, with component
ablations; a per-head MLP; and a position-wise joint MLP of about $37$M
parameters. For the closest published design we implement top-$k$ source-layer
selection with de-RoPE keys \citep{heo2026} in two forms: averaging the selected
layers with $200$ audit contexts, and concatenating them with $100$ calibration
sequences of $1{,}024$ tokens from a generated web-text-style corpus; the
reference uses $500$ sequences (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}).

\textbf{Calibration ladder.} For the strongest linear family we vary the
calibration budget over $c\in\{10,30,60,100,200,500\}$ examples on the same
fixed evaluation set, so that budget effects are not confounded with evaluation
items.

\textbf{Probes.} The fraction probe injects
$\alpha\widehat C_S+(1-\alpha)C_S$ for $\alpha\in\{0.25,0.5,0.75\}$; the window
probe replaces the bottom, middle, or top third of student layers with
translated content; the octant probe replaces one of eight layer windows. All
probes require the student's own prefill, so they are measurement instruments,
not deployable systems.

\textbf{Diagnostics.} We measure held-out reconstruction $R^2$ of the student's
own $K$ and $V$ from the teacher's states with the same fit budget for every
family (20 calibration contexts, 10 disjoint held-out contexts), and we report
\kv{} norm ratios between teacher and student.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{H1: mechanics is not the failure mode}
\label{sec:h1}
The identity injection reproduces the student's own prefill: on the primary pair
the change is $+0.0001$ $[-0.0006,+0.0009]$ with per-sample logit cosine $0.99998$
on average and $0.99986$ at worst ($n{=}30$), and the control holds in every
other run we report, whose mean cosines lie between $0.99998$ and $0.99999$.
The guarantee also survives persistence: an online run that
reloads only the persisted cache and mapper, never the teacher, reproduces the
offline translated and identity results at the recorded precision ($-0.1376$ and
$+0.00013$). The native-cache ablation differs between the two runs because the
online path carries no native cache, so we scope equivalence to the translated
and identity paths. Any deficit below is therefore attributable to the map or to
the content it moves.

\subsection{H2: no tested mapper replaces the strong student's cache}
\label{sec:h2}
Table~\ref{tab:main} collects the mapper ladder. Every strong-student
translation has a negative point estimate relative to the student's own
prefill, from $-0.138$ to $-0.392$, and all but the best configuration are
statistically degraded. The best configuration (per-head affine, $c{=}30$) is
the only one whose interval reaches zero ($-0.138$ $[-0.312,+0.037]$), and it does not
pass the replacement gate.
Three controls show that this is not an artifact of our implementation choices.

\emph{Calibration volume does not close the gap in the tested range.} On one fixed evaluation set the same
family moves between $-0.2569$ and $-0.2378$ across the six budgets, a spread
of $0.02$ against intervals of width $\ge 0.2$.

\emph{Capacity and anchoring do not close the gap in the tested range.} A per-head MLP (five
trainings, range $-0.39$ to $-0.26$) and a $37$M-parameter joint MLP (five
trainings, $-0.30$ to $-0.26$) sit inside the linear band; removing RAT's
analytic anchor raises suffix perplexity from $23.7$ to $501.6$ while changing
CHG from $-0.223$ to $-0.268$---fluency moves, the answer deficit does not.

\emph{Source-layer combination does not close the gap in the tested range.} The closest published design
fails in both forms we can match---its source-layer combination and its
sequence length---while differing in calibration volume and corpus
(Appendix~\ref{app:impl}): averaging selected top-$k$ layers gives $-0.273$,
$-0.308$, and $-0.291$ for $k{=}1,3,5$; concatenating them with long-sequence
web-text calibration gives $-0.279$, $-0.223$, and $-0.254$. All six intervals
lie below zero, and the identity control passes in all six runs.

\emph{The weak student is a different story, but not a positive one.} It reaches
$+0.010$ $[-0.089,+0.106]$, whose interval spans both zero and $-\epsilon$: we
establish neither replacement nor degradation. Two further weak-student rows
behave the same way ($-0.023$ $[-0.106,+0.060]$ with an $8$B teacher; $-0.041$
$[-0.124,+0.038]$ for RAT). The largest point estimate anywhere in the audit is
$+0.010$, and it belongs to this pair.

\subsection{H3: No Teacher-Content Probe Establishes Gain}
\label{sec:h3}
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:probes} reports the probes under the confirmatory
translator (RAT, $c{=}30$) together with a replication under the strongest
linear translator (affine, $c{=}30$).

The fraction probe degrades monotonically with the teacher fraction under both
translators, so adding teacher-derived content to the student's own cache does
not help. The window probe shows a real asymmetry, but not a useful one:
replacing the top third of layers is neutral (its interval contains zero in both
translators), while the middle and bottom thirds are harmful; the ordering
between middle and bottom is translator-dependent, so we claim only that the
top window is neutral.

Over the six confirmatory configurations (three fractions, three windows, RAT
source) we bootstrap the maximum of the mean CHG over evaluation samples
($10^4$ resamples). The observed maximum is $-0.001$ and the $95\%$ upper
estimate for the maximum gain is $+0.009$. This is a percentile bound on a
maximum, not a centered max-$t$ construction; we therefore read it as
``the bootstrap $95\%$ upper estimate of the best achievable gain in this family
is about one gold-probability point'', and not as a simultaneous $95\%$
exclusion statement. Under the affine replication every configuration is again
non-positive, and a best-of-eight octant sweep reaches $+0.020$
$[-0.012,+0.052]$, whose interval contains zero. Across all $48$ probe rows
recorded under the protocol, none has an interval that excludes zero on the
positive side.

\section{Why Reconstruction Is Not Enough}
\label{sec:analysis}

The audit's two most transferable findings are inequalities rather than
mechanisms, and both are supported directly by the recorded runs.

\subsection{Reconstruction fidelity does not imply functional equivalence}
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:r2} reports held-out reconstruction under one fixed
budget (20 calibration contexts, 10 disjoint held-out contexts) for four
configurations.

Two contrasts carry the point. The joint MLP reaches $R^2_V = 0.481$ against the
affine mapper's $0.323$ and still produces a CHG between $-0.30$ and $-0.26$,
inside the band of every other strong-student translator. The choice of
source-layer combination moves reconstruction in opposite directions (averaging
degrades it with $k$; concatenation improves it) while leaving the verdict
unchanged. Whatever the mappers recover does not appear to include the part of
the cache the student needs in order to answer.

\subsection{Fluency does not imply capability}
The same pattern appears on perplexity. Our most fluent failing translator
reaches suffix perplexity $23.7$, within $2.5$ of the identity control's $21.2$,
and still answers $26.7\%$ against the student's $50.0\%$; the joint MLP is
fluent too ($31.6$) with gold probability $0.238$ against $0.503$. Perplexity
tracks whether the cache permits a fluent continuation, accuracy tracks whether
it supports the task, and the two dissociate across the recorded runs---which
matters because perplexity is cheap and commonly reported as a quality proxy.

\subsection{Channel asymmetry}
At $c{=}30$ with the ridge family, mapping only values answers $36.7\%$ of
items, mapping only keys answers $30.0\%$, and mapping both answers $20.0\%$,
while suffix perplexity orders the three in the opposite direction
($1.85\times10^{7}$, $253$, and $88.5$). Mapping keys therefore repairs fluency
while damaging task behavior. We report this as an observation about the tested
channel maps; without attention-map analysis we do not claim to have identified
the mechanism, and the reading we consider most consistent with the pattern is
that the observed asymmetry reflects key-side routing errors.

\subsection{Context length, cross-architecture students, and replay}
The deficit is not specific to short contexts. A four-choice retrieval task
packed to roughly $1$K tokens gives $-0.285$ $[-0.549,-0.003]$ ($n{=}30$);
with caches stored in float16 and the fixed-tail protocol disabled, a true
$4$K run gives $-0.596$ $[-0.939,-0.135]$ and a true $8$K run gives $-0.577$
$[-0.943,-0.082]$ (both $n{=}10$). These two rows are a different evaluation
regime from the $1$K row, and we read them as further degradation rather than as
a calibrated length sweep.

The deficit is also not specific to one architecture family: five alternative
students give three negative rows (Llama-3.2-3B $-0.044$, Gemma-3-1B $-0.061$,
Qwen2.5-1.5B $-0.157$; all $n{=}100$) and two near-parity rows on students
close to chance (Llama-3.2-1B $+0.000$, Gemma-2-2B $-0.009$), while token
alignment leaves the ranking unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}). A naive
target-side re-read, which violates zero re-prefill, does not recover the
student either ($-0.232$ $[-0.335,-0.134]$ against $-0.249$ $[-0.353,-0.149]$);
we did not re-implement any published correction loss, so this bounds a naive
replay rather than those systems.

A text-channel control points the same way: summarizing the teacher's context
into text reaches $0.440$ accuracy and $0.404$ gold against the student's $0.520$
and $0.512$, a difference of $-0.108$ ($n{=}100$).

\subsection{What the audit covers}
The recorded artifacts contain $177$ evaluation runs with metrics and $181$
configuration rows under the audit protocol ($133$ translations, $48$ probes).
The largest translation point estimate anywhere in the audit is $+0.010$
$[-0.089,+0.106]$, from the weak-student pair, and no probe row has an interval
that excludes zero on the positive side.

\section{Implications for Cache-Translation Research}
\label{sec:implications}

The practical consequence is a three-instrument checklist. Report an identity
control that injects the student's own cache through the full pipeline, because
it separates a broken pipeline from a broken map. Report accuracy and not only
retention or perplexity, because retention measures similarity to the receiver's
own prefill rather than the receiver's gain over it, and because fluency and
task quality dissociate in our runs. Report a no-additional-training probe of
teacher-derived content before investing in translator capacity: the probe takes
its content from a fixed, already-calibrated translator and trains nothing new,
and in our audit no probe configuration produces an interval that excludes zero.

The scientific consequence is the two inequalities above: reconstruction quality
and perplexity are not substitutes for task quality, so a translation result
reported only through them is underdetermined. The reading that the teacher's
advantage is mediated by parameter-dependent computation is consistent with our
results---FFN layers act as key--value memories---but we present it as an
interpretation, not an impossibility result, since a translator outside our
tested families might behave differently.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Scale and samples.} Our scale pairs all come from one model family on
the primary axis, with $n$ between $10$ and $100$ evaluation samples per run;
the widths of the intervals reflect that. The $4$K and $8$K retrieval rows use
$n{=}10$, a float16 cache regime, and a different evaluation protocol, so they
qualify the context-length claim rather than establishing a length sweep.

\textbf{One primary pair.} The ladder, the calibration study, and the probes are
all run on Qwen3-4B$\to$1.7B. The cross-architecture check covers five
alternative students but three of them are near chance, so it establishes
mechanics and gate-level replacement rather than capability.

\textbf{Exploitability is tested, not exhausted.} Our probes cover linear
blending, layer-window replacement, and an octant sweep under two translators. A
nonlinear or jointly structured extraction path outside this family would not be
detected, and the null result is evidence about the tested interventions rather
than a proof of impossibility. A positive probe result would have been decisive;
ours is not.

\textbf{Gradient-trained families are seed-dependent and not always
reproducible.} The per-head MLP is the clearest case: two runs with the same
configuration and the same recorded seed differ by $0.046$ in CHG
($-0.303$ versus $-0.257$). We therefore report those families as ranges over
trainings, and we do not treat any single MLP number as a reproducible point
estimate. The joint MLP reproduces its seed to within $0.001$.

\textbf{The reporting margin is not pre-registered.} $\epsilon{=}0.02$ was
chosen after the gate form was frozen, as a practical reporting margin; the
sensitivity of our conclusions to that choice is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\textbf{What remains unmeasured.} The cold-start equivalence rests on a single
offline/online pair and covers the translated and identity paths only. The
held-out reconstruction $R^2$ comes from one fit of 20 contexts with 10 held-out
contexts, so it constrains rather than characterizes what a mapper can recover.
Our implementation of the closest published design matches the reference's
combination and sequence length; it uses $100$ calibration sequences instead of
$500$, drawn from a generated corpus. We report no deployment-cost claim: the
recorded pipeline measure includes a measurement-only forward pass and is
negative ($-9.7$ at best).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Under strict zero re-prefill, no tested translator establishes replacement for
our primary strong-student pair, and the only rows that clear the reporting
replacement margin sit on near-chance alternative students and show no
measurable gain. The injection pipeline itself is exact to numerical
tolerance, so the failure is not mechanical: the deficit on the primary pair
persists across the tested calibration
budgets, translator capacities, source-layer combinations, and token-alignment
controls; and it is not repaired by matching the closest published design's
source-layer composition and calibration sequence length under our generated
calibration corpus and $100$-sequence volume. The
two findings we expect to outlast this setting are the decouplings: cache
reconstruction quality and cache-induced fluency are not substitutes for task
quality. We release the runs, the protocol, and the instruments so that future
translation claims can be checked against all three.

\section{Reproducibility and Recorded Inventory}
\label{app:repro}

Every number in this paper comes from a recorded run directory containing the
run's configuration, per-sample scores, the git hash, the protocol version, and
a zero-re-prefill verification flag. At the time of writing the repository
contains $177$ evaluation runs with metrics and $181$ configuration rows under
the audit protocol ($133$ translations, $48$ probes). A re-run audit of $23$
core configurations reproduced the deterministic families to within $|\Delta|
\le 0.002$ in CHG; the per-head MLP family did not reproduce across runs with
the same seed (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), which is why it is reported as a
range. For the gradient-trained families the runs set Python, NumPy, and PyTorch
seeds (CPU and CUDA) but do not enable deterministic kernel selection, so the
residual source of nondeterminism has not been isolated.

The margin sensitivity referenced in Section~\ref{sec:framework} is recorded in
the same directory: conclusions about the strong-student pair are unchanged for
$\epsilon\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05\}$ because their intervals lie below zero, while
the weak-student pair fails the replacement gate under all three.

Intervals are recomputed from those per-sample scores with $10^4$ paired
percentile bootstrap resamples (\texttt{scripts/bootstrap\_ci\_report.py},
artifact \texttt{reports/bootstrap\_1e4/ci\_1e4.json}); an earlier draft's
$10^3$-draw endpoints differ by up to $0.02$, the Monte-Carlo noise of $10^3$
draws at these sample sizes, so the $10^4$-draw values are reported.
The identity gate's \emph{form} was fixed before the runs, while the numerical
tolerance $0.9998$ was introduced during revision as a reproducible
operationalization of the original ``within numerical tolerance'' criterion;
the recorded runs store logit cosines rather than logits, so the residual
identity difference (minimum $0.99986$) is not decomposed further.
